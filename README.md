# Russia OSINT

Public records, open data, sanctions lists and tools for investigating stories about Russia. Every link was checked on the date shown beside it.

**167 sources. Last full check: 2026-11-19.** 114 loaded for us; 53 could not be reached from our checker and are marked in the table. Many Russian government sites block or drop connections from outside Russia, and some aggregators and research sites block automated checks, so a source marked that way may still work for you, from a browser or from inside the country.

## How to read the tables

- **Access** says what stands between you and the data: *Open* (nothing), *Free login*, *Captcha* (free, but a captcha on each search), *Paid*, or *Partly paid*.
- **Run by** tells you whose data it is. A Russian government portal, a commercial aggregator, a foreign government's sanctions list and an independent newsroom each have different reasons to be right or wrong.
- **Checked** is the date we last opened the link. Portals move, rename and get blocked often. If the date is old, check the link before relying on it.
- Notes in *italics* are caveats we hit while testing.

## What is deliberately left out

This list covers records about companies, government, courts, public spending, land, the environment, infrastructure, sanctions, the military and transport. It does not include people-search services, phone, passport or vehicle-owner lookups, Telegram "probiv" bots, or anything built on leaked or breached personal data. Those are not open sources, and using them can put both you and the person at risk. The exceptions are clearly marked: archives where newsrooms publish leaked documents after editorial review, such as OCCRP Aleph and the ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database.

## Using these sources well

- **A record shows what was filed, not what is true.** Company filings, declarations and tender documents are claims made by the filer.
- **Archive what you find.** Russian pages change and disappear without notice. Save them to the Wayback Machine or archive.today and keep a dated copy.
- **Names are not unique, and transliterations vary.** Match on a second detail (an INN or OGRN number, an address, a date) before saying two records are the same person or company.
- **Think about safety.** Contacting people in Russia, or working with organisations labelled "foreign agents", "undesirable", "extremist" or "terrorist", can carry legal risks for them and for you. Check the law before you scrape.

## Contributing

Corrections matter more than additions. If a link has moved, a portal has started charging, or a description is wrong, please open an issue or a pull request.

To add a source, add an object to `research/entries.json` with these fields and run `python3 build_readme.py`:

```json
{
  "category": "Courts and law",
  "name": "Name of the source",
  "url": "https://...",
  "what": "One sentence on what a journalist can find there.",
  "access": "open | free login | captcha | paid | partly paid",
  "run_by": "Who runs it",
  "status": "ok | unreachable | changed",
  "checked": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "note": "Optional caveat"
}
```

Please open the link yourself on the day you add it, and keep to the scope above: no people-search services and nothing built on leaked data.
